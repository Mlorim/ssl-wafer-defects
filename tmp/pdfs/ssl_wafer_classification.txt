  ABSTRACT The semiconductor industry faces significant challenges in wafer quality assessment due
  to the increasing complexity of device fabrication and the rise in inline defects and circuit failures.
  As manufacturing processes advance, conventional manual analysis of wafer bin maps (WBMs) has
  become impractical and unreliable, unable to keep pace with the industry’s demands. While convolutional
  neural networks (CNNs) are widely used in automated systems for classifying WBM defect patterns, their
  dependence on supervised learning limits their effectiveness, as they cannot utilize the vast amounts of
  unlabeled WBM data. Additionally, class imbalances in WBM datasets often result in models biased toward
  majority classes, leading to suboptimal performance. In this study, we produce ClimEx, a semi-supervised
  learning framework that addresses the extreme class imbalance problem commonly found in WBM datasets
  through consistency regularization and pseudo-labeling. Our method maintains dynamic per-class thresholds
  to correct the imbalance in pseudo-labels caused by biased predictions, ensuring balanced representation
  across classes even under extreme imbalance. On a publicly available benchmark, ClimEx demonstrates
  improved predictive performance compared to state-of-the-art methods. We also provide a comprehensive
  analysis of the key components of our method from multiple perspectives.
